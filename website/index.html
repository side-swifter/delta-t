Hybrid air + liquid cooling · Conrad Challenge 2026–27

Cool the rack by the dew point.

Older data centers are bolting liquid cooling onto air-cooled halls to keep up with AI. Delta-T is a controller that decides, second by second, how much heat the fans and the liquid loop should each carry. It picks the split that uses the least power while keeping every chip under 70 °C and the coolant above the dew point.

01 · The constraint

Liquid is cheaper per watt. Condensation is not.

A liquid loop moves heat far more efficiently than fans, so the obvious move is to run it as cold as it goes. But if the coolant drops below the dew point of the room air, water condenses on the cold plates, right on top of live electronics.

The dew point follows the weather. So the coldest safe coolant temperature keeps moving, and so does the best split between air and liquid. Building systems designed for all-air halls don't track it.

02 · Why the split matters

Fans pay in cubes.

Fan power rises with roughly the cube of fan speed. Every bit of heat the liquid loop can safely take lets the fans slow down, and a small slowdown is a large saving. That trade is what the controller is optimizing.

03 · How it works

Four layers, running every second.

  1. L1

    Log everything

    Chip, coolant, inlet and exhaust temperatures, humidity, flow and power for each part, logged once a second. Every claim we make later rests on this.

    1 Hz · append-only CSV · sequence checked

  2. L2

    Fit a thermal model

    A lumped model of each node: its heat capacity, linked to the air stream and the coolant by conductances that change with fan speed and flow. Fitted to logs and checked on data it never saw.

    Q = h·A·ΔT · Q = ṁ·cp·ΔT

  3. L3

    Predict the load

    A small time-series model forecasts server load 30 to 120 seconds ahead, trained on public Google and Alibaba cluster traces and our own recordings. It runs on the Pi.

    30–120 s ahead · edge only, no cloud

  4. L4

    Pick the cheapest safe split

    Each control step searches fan and pump settings for the lowest total power that keeps every chip under its limit and the coolant above the dew point plus a margin.

    Tchip < 70 °C · Tcoolant > Tdew + 2 °C

04 · The rig

A real rack. Real water. Real humidity.

A single-rack testbed built like a messy real retrofit: fans for the whole rack, a liquid loop on some of the nodes, and a sealed enclosure so we can sweep humidity on purpose.

Heat

Cartridge heaters in CNC aluminum blocks

They replay recorded server workloads watt for watt, so every test run sees the same load.

Liquid

PWM DDC pump and cold plates

Aluminum cold plates, an aluminum radiator and inhibited coolant, so no mixed metals corrode the loop.

Chill

Peltier chiller stage

Two TEC1-12706 modules pull coolant below room temperature. Without them the dew point limit would never come into play.

Sense

SHT31 humidity and temperature

Accurate enough to compute the dew point we steer against, alongside DS18B20 probes on every node and both ends of the loop.

Control

ESP32-S3 and Raspberry Pi 5

The ESP32 drives PWM and reads sensors. The Pi runs the model and the optimizer, entirely offline.

Safety

Fail-safes the software can't override

KSD301 thermal fuses and a leak sensor on a hardware relay. The loop is leak-tested unpowered for 24 hours before any heat goes in.

08 · FAQ

Questions judges ask.

Isn't this just a thermostat?

A thermostat holds one temperature. Delta-T chooses between two cooling systems with different costs, under two limits that move: chip temperature and the dew point. The best answer changes with the load and the weather.

Why not use reinforcement learning?

We don't need it, and it would be harder to trust. A fitted physical model plus a small search over fan and pump settings is fast, explainable, and easy to check against the safety limits.

How much energy does it save?

We don't know yet, and we won't guess. The headline number will be measured on the rig: total cooling energy for the same workload at equal safety, against a properly tuned standard controller.

What happens if the controller fails?

Thermal fuses and a leak sensor on a hardware relay cut power without asking the software. The controller is never the last line of defense.

Who is this for?

Operators of older air-cooled data centers who are adding liquid cooling for AI hardware and have to run both systems together in the same room.
